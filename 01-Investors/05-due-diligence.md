## Why Did the Entrepreneur say $400,000 When the Table Shows $260,000?
> *Pitch numbers are claims, but due diligence turns them into verified facts.*

- The entrepreneur probably gave an annualized revenue run rate, while the table shows the actual revenue earned
- For example, the SaaS timber company may get most of its customers during Christmas time in December
- The entrepreneur may have projected their December revenue: `DecemberRevenue × 12 = $400,000 annualized`
- But because those customers were not active for the entire year, the company actually recorded only $260,000
- The difference, $140,000, is not missing money per se, but rather may represent revenue that would have existed if the end-of-year customer base had been active for the entire year
- **Did the entrepreneur lie?** Not necessarily. However, saying “we make $400,000” without identifying it as an annualized run rate would be misleading to the investors at hand
- The investor should request
    - Monthly revenue records
    - Customer start dates
    - Signed contracts
    - Invoices and cash receipts
    - Recurring versus one-time revenue
    - Customer and truck counts
    - Cancellations and churn
